# PART 14 — CAUCUS STRATEGY (MOTIONS, BLOC-BUILDING, NEGOTIATION)

## 14.1 Motion strategy table
| Motion | When | Why (your purpose) |
|---|---|---|
| Moderated caucus: "Negotiation mandate and timeline for the CCW Review Conference" | First caucus block | **Own the agenda's spine.** Forces every delegation to take a position on your core demand; signals you as the drafter. 90–120s total, 30s speaking time. |
| Moderated caucus: "The two-tier approach: prohibitions and regulations" | After GSL round 1 | Your substantive home turf; lets African Group + EU speak your language; flush out maximalists and blockers. |
| Moderated caucus: "Meaningful human control: content and standards" | Mid-committee | Your operator's credibility; moves debate from slogans to text — where you are strongest. |
| Moderated caucus: "Capacity building and African participation" | Before unmod | Recruits the Global South formally; sets up your working-paper chapter. |
| Moderated caucus: "Role of voluntary measures and transparency" | When Bloc B pushes its track | Pre-empts the rival bloc: discuss their content *inside your framework*. |
| Unmoderated caucus | Immediately after first GSL round | Bloc building + working paper drafting. See 14.3. |
| Motion to introduce working paper | As soon as your draft is coherent | Be the reference text. |
| Right of reply | Only on personal attacks | Max 2 uses; discipline is a feature. |
| Motion to table/divide | Only if Bloc B tries a poison-pill amendment | Procedural kill shot; use sparingly. |

## 14.2 Bloc-building sequence (unmod #1 and #2)
1. **Before committee:** identify 3–4 delegates (Austria, Sierra Leone, Brazil, Mexico/Germany) — approach in the first 10 minutes, before the first GSL if possible.
2. **Unmod #1 (first 20 min):** (a) Sierra Leone + Nigeria + 2–3 African states: African Group statement continuity + joint drafting; (b) Austria + Ireland/Netherlands: merge your mandate language with the 42-state statement; (c) Brazil: rolling-text language; (d) Mexico/Costa Rica: anti-personnel prohibition as negotiation-agenda item (channel maximalists); (e) Türkiye: producers' consultative idea (private, low-key).
3. **Unmod #2 (after Bloc B tables its text):** merger negotiation — your draft as the skeleton; offer Bloc B: their transparency/legal-review content as an implementation chapter (your counterattack from Part 12, Attack #2), in exchange for: no deletion of the negotiation mandate; no "political declaration only" language.
4. **Throughout:** keep a written "commitment tracker" (delegate → what they promised → what you owe them). This is the note-taking habit of winning delegates.

## 14.3 What you can offer in negotiation (concrete offers only)
- Co-sponsorship slots on the working paper/draft resolution;
- The anti-personnel prohibition *as a negotiation-agenda priority* (for maximalists);
- "Producers' consultative forum" language (for Turkey/China/ROK/UK);
- Capacity-building clauses with named implementing partners (UNIDIR, UNODA, UNDP) and voluntary-funding architecture (for Global South);
- Preambular recognition of voluntary declarations and REAIM (for US-leaning delegates);
- A 2028 target with 2027 interim review (for realists on timeline).

## 14.4 What you can never offer
- Deletion of the negotiation mandate; deletion of meaningful human control as the standard; singling-out language (any state); an unconditional ban on armed drones; exclusion of African states from negotiation structures.

## 14.5 Bloc leadership role — verdict: **Technical Drafter + Coalition Broker**, not loudest voice.
**Why:** your bridge position is most credible as the writer of the synthesis text. The visible-leader slot will be contested (Austria/Mexico/US); the drafter slot is uncontested, earns the chair's respect, and produces the artifact everyone votes on. You speak in every substantive caucus — but your interventions are short, textual, and offer the next line of the draft. That is the profile chairs remember for Best Delegate.
