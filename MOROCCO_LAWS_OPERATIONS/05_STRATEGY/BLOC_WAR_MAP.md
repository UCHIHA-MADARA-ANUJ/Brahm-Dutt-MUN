# PART 9 — BLOC WAR MAP (PREDICT THE ROOM, THEN SHAPE IT)

## 9.1 Predicted blocs
**Bloc A — "Negotiation Now" (majority):** ~100–130 states: African Group core, EU majority (Austria-led), LAC, CARICOM, Pacific, NAM/OIC (minus abstainers), the 42-state group. *Core demand:* binding instrument; two-tier; rolling text basis; Review Conference mandate. *Strongest argument:* ten years of elements; SG/ICRC urgency; proliferation reality; 164-vote majority. *Weakest point:* no consensus mechanism inside CCW; anti-personnel prohibition splits it; timeline unrealistic if forced to 2026. *Likely sponsors:* Austria, Sierra Leone, Brazil, Mexico, New Zealand, Ireland, + you.
**Bloc B — "Regulation First / Voluntary" (rival):** US-led; joined by like-minded producers (Japan, ROK, Australia, parts of NATO), Israel, UK (soft). *Core demand:* political declaration + national measures + transparency; no negotiation mandate. *Strongest argument:* definitions immature; consensus fragile; unilateral disarmament risk; technology gap. *Weakest point:* they are the minority (6–15 states); their "no new law" position contradicts their own national rules (DoD 3000.09, UK legal reviews).
**Bloc C — "Blockers":** Russia, Belarus, DPRK (+ Burundi opportunistically). *Core demand:* nothing binding; narrow definitions; CCW consensus as veto. *Weakest point:* isolation; contradiction (Russia's rhetoric on military AI superiority vs blocking rules).
**Bloc D — "Humanitarian Maximalists" (inside Bloc A):** Mexico, Costa Rica, CARICOM, HRW-aligned delegates, possibly South Africa/Malawi: demand anti-personnel prohibition + immediate ban language. *Risk:* their maximalism can fragment Bloc A; *opportunity:* their energy supplies the urgency narrative — channel it into the negotiation mandate, not into a doomed "ban everything" text.
**Bloc E — "African Solidarity" (your base):** African Group positions (LBI, human control, capacity). Note the fracture lines: Burundi (2025 no), abstainers (Madagascar, Niger, South Sudan), Algeria (will likely agree with you on LAWS but attack you on Western Sahara — expect tactical strangeness).

## 9.2 How to break, expand, or redirect
- **Expand Bloc A into Bloc B's middle:** put "national legal reviews, testing/evaluation, transparency, training" — the US declaration's own content — *inside* your binding-instrument framework as the implementation chapter. Then ask: "if the United States' own 10 measures are good enough for a declaration, why are they not good enough for a treaty chapter?" This is your strongest anti-US move: **adopt their language, then out-lawyer them.**
- **Redirect Bloc D:** agree that the *negotiations* must address the anti-personnel prohibition (ICRC position) as a first-priority item in the negotiation agenda, without pre-committing the outcome in this resolution. That keeps maximalists inside your tent without making the resolution hostage.
- **Isolate Bloc C procedurally:** on every vote, force recorded votes; use explanations of vote to name the blocking dynamic ("one state's veto of progress in Geneva cannot veto humanity's need for law" — without naming states in a hostile way; the policy, not the person).
- **Your expansion asset:** the producers' consultative voice. Propose in unmod: "producer and operator states should have a dedicated seat in the negotiation structure" — Turkey, China, US, Israel, ROK cannot easily vote against a seat at the table; it de-fangs Bloc B/C while preserving your credibility with them.
