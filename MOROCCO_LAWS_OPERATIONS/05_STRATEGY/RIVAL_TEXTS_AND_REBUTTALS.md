# ⚔️ RIVAL TEXTS & REBUTTALS — THE FOUR RESOLUTIONS YOU'LL FACE (SESSION 6)
**Pre-drafted for the four rival archetypes. Know them before they're tabled. Each: predicted clauses → their best arguments → your rebuttal lines → counter-amendment.**

---

## RIVAL #1: THE US-STYLE "VOLUNTARY FRAMEWORK" RESOLUTION
**Sponsor likely:** United States (+ maybe Japan/ROK/Australia-leaning delegates, some NATO)
**Core thesis:** existing IHL sufficient; national measures + political declaration + transparency; no binding instrument now.
**Predicted clauses:**
- PP: welcomes the Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI (2023), REAIM outcomes, national policies
- OP: "Encourages States to implement voluntary measures..."; "Encourages continued exchanges within the CCW"; "Reaffirms that IHL applies"; notably ABSENT: negotiation mandate, prohibitions, dates
**Their strongest arguments:**
1. "The technology is immature; a treaty would freeze bad definitions."
2. "Voluntary measures work faster than treaties (US declaration, REAIM, national legal reviews)."
3. "The US's own 10 measures ARE responsible-use governance."
4. "A treaty without the major powers is a paper treaty."
**Your rebuttals (in order):**
1. "The UN Panel of Experts documented autonomous attack in Libya in 2020. Türkiye fired a 20-drone autonomous swarm in January. The technology is not immature — the law is."
2. "Voluntary measures are gifts of the current administration; treaties are promises of the state. REAIM and the declaration are excellent — that is why they belong in a treaty chapter, not instead of one."
3. "We agree with the United States' ten measures so strongly that we put them in our text as binding obligations. The question is not whether the measures are good; it is whether they should be enforceable."
4. "The Ottawa Convention began without three major powers and now binds 162. We build the norm; universalization follows."
**Counter-amendment (if tabled):** your DA-1 ("Recognizing that voluntary and national measures contribute to responsible military use of artificial intelligence, the General Assembly affirms... the urgent need for a legally binding international instrument...") — converts their preamble into your preamble.
**Verdict:** beat it with OP7 (their own content, binding) + OP2 (the date) + recorded votes.

## RIVAL #2: THE MAXIMALIST "BAN EVERYTHING NOW" RESOLUTION
**Sponsor likely:** Mexico, Costa Rica, Pakistan, HRW-aligned delegates, possibly South Africa
**Core thesis:** machines must never kill; immediate comprehensive ban on development/production/use; moratorium now.
**Predicted clauses:**
- PP: Martens Clause; right to life; digital dehumanization; "killer robots"
- OP: "Prohibits the development, production and use of all autonomous weapons systems"; "Calls for an immediate global moratorium"; "Establishes a verification mechanism"
**Their strongest arguments:**
1. "Human dignity is non-negotiable — machines deciding who lives is abhorrent."
2. "Prevention is cheaper than repair (Protocol IV precedent)."
3. "A moratorium is the only honest interim step."
**Your rebuttals:**
1. "We agree with every word of the moral case — which is exactly why the prohibition layer of our text exists. The disagreement is not about dignity; it is about scope. Ban the function — targeting without human control — and you ban the horror while keeping every lawful tool. Ban everything and you will be negotiated by no one and ratified by fewer."
2. "Protocol IV banned blinding lasers because they had no military utility and caused gratuitous harm. Autonomous ISR, logistics and defense systems have utility and can comply with IHL. The analogy supports prohibition of the unacceptable — not prohibition of everything."
3. "A moratorium without a negotiation mandate is a gesture; ours comes with a calendar. Join our text and the moratorium becomes law-in-progress; split the majority and it becomes a press release."
**Counter-amendment:** your FA-1 (anti-personnel prohibition as negotiation priority) + OP6bis moratorium — their energy, your structure.
**Verdict:** court them, don't fight them. Their moral force is your preamble; their scope is your negotiation agenda.

## RIVAL #3: THE RUSSIAN "NO ACTION / CCW-ONLY" RESOLUTION
**Sponsor likely:** Russia (+ Belarus, DPRK, Burundi, maybe Syria)
**Core thesis:** premature; definitions unclear; CCW is the only forum; no GA interference.
**Predicted clauses:**
- PP: CCW/GGE primacy; "existing intergovernmental process"
- OP: "Decides that discussions shall continue exclusively within the CCW"; "Calls upon States to refrain from negotiating outside the CCW"; no mandate, no dates
**Their strongest arguments:**
1. "The CCW is the right forum — the GA is politicizing a technical issue."
2. "No agreed definition = no basis for a treaty."
3. "Consensus protects everyone."
**Your rebuttals:**
1. "We agree the CCW is the right forum — our text sends the mandate to the CCW's own Review Conference. What we reject is the right forum becoming the right forever."
2. "The GGE has a characterization; the rolling text has elements; the ICRC has a framework. 'No definition' was true in 2016. It is a choice now."
3. "Consensus is a result, not a method. Ten years of consensus produced eleven principles and zero negotiations. The majority of humanity has a right to a calendar."
**Counter-amendment:** your DA-2 (non-prejudicial clause — "Nothing in this resolution shall be construed as prejudging the rules of procedure of the CCW") — removes their procedural objection while keeping the mandate.
**Verdict:** don't persuade; isolate. Recorded votes + explanation-of-vote naming the dynamic without naming states.

## RIVAL #4: THE CHINESE "CCW-FIRST, NARROW PROHIBITION" RESOLUTION
**Sponsor likely:** China (+ like-minded)
**Core thesis:** negotiate within CCW; prohibit only "unacceptable" systems (5 cumulative criteria); no R&D restrictions; no GA-convened conference.
**Predicted clauses:**
- PP: CCW primacy; dual-use; sovereignty over R&D
- OP: "Calls upon the GGE to continue..."; "Prohibits the use of fully autonomous lethal weapons systems" (note: USE, not development); "Nothing shall restrict research and development"
**Their strongest arguments:**
1. "Prohibition should be precise — five criteria avoid overreach."
2. "R&D is legitimate; only use is problematic."
3. "CCW consensus preserves universality."
**Your rebuttals:**
1. "China's five criteria are a serious contribution — and they are the same architecture as the two-tier approach: prohibit the unacceptable, regulate the rest. Let us put both texts on the table in negotiations and reconcile them. That is what a negotiation is for."
2. "If only use is prohibited, every state can build the system and claim it will 'use it responsibly.' The ATT and the CWC regulate transfer and development precisely because capability becomes use."
3. "Consensus that preserves the right to veto is consensus that preserves the status quo."
**Counter-amendment:** acknowledge their five-criteria proposal in your preambular; add OP10 savings clause (already in your text) — their R&D protection is your OP10.
**Verdict:** recruit to abstention-not-opposition; never force a public confrontation; OP2 (CCW-first mandate) + OP10 are your bridge to them.

## THE MERGER SEQUENCE (when the room has 2–3 drafts)
1. **Do not merge with Rival #3** (Russia) — nothing to gain, everything to lose.
2. **Merge with Rival #1** (US-style): take their implementation chapter (legal reviews, T&E, transparency, voluntary measures as complementary preamble) → your OP5/OP7 already contains it; give them FA-4 (human rights preamble) + DA-2 (non-prejudicial clause) → keep OP2/OP3 (mandate + fallback).
3. **Merge with Rival #2** (maximalists): take their anti-personnel prohibition + moratorium → your FA-1 + OP6bis → keep two-tier structure and OP10 (civilian AI).
4. **Merge with Rival #4** (China): take their five-criteria acknowledgment + R&D protection (already OP10) → keep the mandate and the GA fallback clause.
5. **Final text = your skeleton + their language.** You remain the drafter of record → unmod score + overall impact.
