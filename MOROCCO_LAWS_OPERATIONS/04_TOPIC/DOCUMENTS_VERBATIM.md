## 1. THE 11 GUIDING PRINCIPLES (CCW GGE, 2019) — verbatim from official submissions
The GGE "endorsed eleven guiding principles by consensus." Key principles (a–d, i–k) as cited in official France/ICRC submissions:

- **(a)** "International humanitarian law continues to apply fully to all weapons systems, including the potential development and use of lethal autonomous weapons systems."
- **(b)** "Human responsibility for decisions on the use of weapons systems must be retained since accountability cannot be transferred to machines. This should be considered across the entire life cycle of the weapons system."
- **(c)** "Human–machine interaction, which may take various forms and be implemented at various stages of the life cycle of a weapon, should ensure that the potential use of weapons systems based on emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapons systems is in compliance with applicable international law, in particular international humanitarian law. In determining the quality and extent of human–machine interaction, a range of factors should be considered including the operational context, and the characteristics and capabilities of the weapons system as a whole."
- **(d)** "Accountability for developing, deploying and using any emerging weapons system in the framework of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons must be ensured in accordance with applicable international law, including through the operation of such systems within a responsible chain of human command and control."
- **(e)** — legal review of new weapons (in ICRC's reading: "obligations to conduct legal reviews of new weapons").
- **(f)** — transfer considerations ("obligations for States Parties... demand consideration in the transfer of weapons").
- **(g)** — "Risk assessments and mitigation measures during the design, development, testing and deployment of new weapons."
- **(i)** "In crafting potential policy measures, emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapons systems should not be anthropomorphized." (Legal obligations rest with humans, not machines.)
- **(j)** "Discussions and any potential policy measures taken within the context of the CCW should not hamper progress in or access to peaceful uses of intelligent autonomous technologies."
- **(k)** "The CCW offers an appropriate framework for dealing with the issue..." (balance between military necessity and humanitarian considerations).

**Your debate uses:**
- (b)+(d) = "Human responsibility and accountability can never be transferred to machines" — the consensus floor, agreed by ALL states including the US/Russia. "These principles were adopted by consensus in 2019 — including by the states now blocking negotiations. What changed? Not the law. The lobby."
- (j) = your OP10 savings clause authority: "The Guiding Principles themselves protect peaceful uses of autonomous technologies. Our OP10 is not an innovation — it is principle (j)."

## 2. NAM STATEMENTS (verbatim) — Morocco's bloc
- **NAM Geneva Chapter working paper (2021, Venezuela on behalf of NAM):** "there is an urgent need to pursue a legally binding instrument under the Convention that will contain prohibitions and regulations for addressing the humanitarian and international security challenges posed by emerging technologies in the area of LAWS."
- **NAM at First Committee (Indonesia, Oct 2021):** "NAM is of the view that lethal autonomous weapon systems should be thoroughly deliberated and examined in the context of conformity to international law."
- **NAM at First Committee (Indonesia, Oct 2020):** "urgent need to pursue a legally binding instrument."
- **Debate line:** "The Non-Aligned Movement — 120 states, including Morocco — has called for a legally binding instrument with prohibitions and regulations since 2020. The majority has been ready for six years. The minority has been ready never."

## 3. SG REPORT A/79/88 — the "moral line" quote
- Para ~90: "**The autonomous targeting of humans by machines is a moral line that must not be crossed.**"
- Also: "time is running out for the international community to take preventive action on this issue."
- Two-tier definition from the report: prohibit "lethal autonomous weapons systems that function without human control or oversight and that cannot be used in compliance with international humanitarian law, and regulate all other types of autonomous weapons systems."
- **Debate use:** "The Secretary-General — the voice of this Organization — has named the moral line. We are not asking this committee to discover it. We are asking it to draw it."

## 4. COSTA RICA'S FORMULATION (submission to A/79/88) — clean maximalist-bridge language
Calling for negotiations, the instrument should enforce:
- "(a) Prohibitions on weapons systems that would target people;"
- "(b) Prohibitions on weapons systems that cannot be used with meaningful human control;"
- "(c) Regulations on all other weapons systems to ensure that they are effectively controlled, understood and limited in the area and duration of their use;"
- "(d) Include regulations to prevent the proliferation and unauthorized use of autonomous weapons."
**Debate use:** "Costa Rica has given us the cleanest formulation of the two-tier approach on record: prohibit targeting people; prohibit no-human-control; regulate everything else with limits on area and duration. That formulation is in our text."

## 5. VIENNA 2024 CHAIR'S SUMMARY (verbatim lines)
- "**Human control must prevail in the use of force.**"
- "The delegation to machines of decisions over choice of targets and life and death is an issue that concerns all of us. It profoundly challenges our global social contract and the UN Charter."
- "**Targeting people is a most pressing ethical issue.**"
- "Distance and dehumanisation, the risks of lowering the political threshold to use force as well as escalation risks, including by machine to machine interaction, raise further concerns."
- "Technology should empower people, not dehumanize them."
- Austria's FM Schallenberg: "humanity is at the crossroads — we must not miss the right turn."
- **Debate use:** "In Vienna, 144 states — including Morocco — endorsed the proposition that human control must prevail in the use of force. The consensus is not in Geneva or New York; it is in the record."

## 6. ICRC MARCH 2026 (verbatim concepts)
- New instrument must "prohibit unpredictable AWS, whose functioning and effects cannot be sufficiently understood, predicted or explained, and AWS designed or used to target humans directly (anti-personnel AWS)."
- "Restrict all other AWS through limits on the type of targets; limits on the duration, scale and geographic scope of operation; limits on the situations of use...; and requirements for effective human supervision, timely intervention and deactivation, or, where necessary, self-destruction or self-neutralization."
- Instrument's purpose: "not only restating existing international humanitarian law (IHL) rules but also clarifying how those rules apply in the context of autonomous weapons and articulating additional specific measures that respond to the particular challenges raised by such systems." (ICRC at GGE, Mar 2026)
- **Debate use:** "The ICRC — custodian of the Geneva Conventions — says existing law is not enough. When the ICRC says 'additional specific measures,' the 'IHL is sufficient' argument is no longer an argument; it is a position against the Red Cross."

## 7. STATE-POSITION TALLY (Future of Life Institute, Sept 2024)
- **119 states support** negotiating a legally binding instrument; 59 undeclared; **10 oppose**.
- African Group supports "at the earliest"; South Africa warns against settling for a weak instrument; China supports LBI "if and when conditions are ripe" — "the treaty framework is the right vessel."
- **Debate use:** "One hundred nineteen states support a legally binding instrument; ten oppose. The debate is not between two worlds — it is between 119 states and the patience of the other 10."
