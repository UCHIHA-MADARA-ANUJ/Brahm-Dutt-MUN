# PART 16 — Q&A WAR ROOM (QUESTIONS THEY WILL ASK YOU — AND ANSWERS)

Format: Q → BEST ANSWER (30s) / SHORT (10s) / AGGRESSIVE (use once, strategically) / IF UNKNOWN (never fabricate).

1. **"Does Morocco support a ban on all autonomous weapons?"** → Best: "Morocco supports a legally binding instrument on a two-tier basis: prohibiting systems that select and engage targets without meaningful human control or IHL compliance, and regulating all others. A blanket ban is neither negotiable nor necessary; human control is." / Unknown-handler: "On specific systems, the characterization is a negotiation matter — we support deciding it in negotiations, not in this room by fiat."
2. **"Define 'meaningful human control'."** → "Context-appropriate human judgment over targeting, with predictability, explainability, accountability, training, testing and legal review — the elements already in the GGE rolling text and in the REAIM implementation measures Morocco endorsed." 
3. **"Why a binding convention and not the political declaration?"** → "Because declarations are gifts of the current administration; treaties are promises of the state. The US declaration's own ten measures are excellent — that is why we implement them and why they belong in a treaty chapter."
4. **"Who pays for capacity building?"** → "Voluntary contributions to a dedicated UNODA/UNIDIR trust fund, donor pledges at the Review Conference, and South–South cooperation — Morocco stands ready to contribute training and expertise, as it has done through IAEA and OPCW channels."
5. **"How is this enforced?"** → "Through national implementation — legal reviews, testing, reporting — and through review conferences, exactly as the CCW and CWC work. Verification of design is more tractable than verification of use, which is why we start there."
6. **"Are autonomous weapons not already prohibited by IHL?"** → "The rules apply — and that is precisely the problem: rules written for human judgment give no answer to a system with no human judgment. A law that applies to everyone and binds no one is a law that protects no one. We need the specific rules."
7. **"Doesn't a treaty hurt your own drone industry?"** → "We operate and will produce drones — human-controlled drones, which the treaty protects and legitimizes. What it restricts is uncontrolled autonomy. The treaty does not hurt our industry; it stabilizes the market by drawing the line every producer can plan around."
8. **"Why November 2026? Why not wait for full consensus in the GGE?"** → "Consensus is not a method — it is a result. Ten years produced elements precisely because consensus was the ceiling. The Review Conference can adopt the mandate by the decision rule of the CCW; negotiations remain open to all. Waiting for unanimity is how a minority schedules the world."
9. **"Your position is basically the EU's — are you Europe's voice in Africa?"** → "The African Group statement of 4 March 2026 — delivered on behalf of African CCW states parties, including Morocco — predates and outruns any European text. We coordinate with our continent first; we are honored that others agree with Africa."
10. **"What about the anti-personnel prohibition the ICRC demands?"** → "We support placing it at the top of the negotiation agenda. Human-controlled systems engaging persons are governed by existing IHL; autonomous systems designed to target persons without human control are precisely the category the two-tier approach prohibits."
11. **"Will China/Russia/US ever join?"** → "Universalization is the goal; it is not the precondition. The landmine ban and the ATT began without some major powers and grew. We build the norm now, and we keep the door open — that is why the negotiation must be inclusive."
12. **"What concrete AI use does Morocco defend?"** → "Intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance, logistics, decision-support, cybersecurity, border surveillance, disaster response — Morocco has set these out in writing in its official submission under resolution 79/239. None of these involves a machine choosing a target without a human."
13. **"Aren't you just protecting your Turkish/Israeli/Chinese suppliers?"** → "We protect a principle: lawful access to defensive technology for developing states, under transparent rules. If that protects our suppliers, it protects every African state's right to defend itself."
14. **"What happens if the Review Conference ignores your resolution?"** → "Then our fallback clause activates: this Assembly convenes an inclusive diplomatic conference in 2027. The GA has done this before — for landmines, cluster munitions, the ATT. November is the first door; it is not the only door."
15. **"How do you verify a system is 'unpredictable'?"** → "Through legal review at design and procurement — testing, evaluation, validation, verification — the same disciplines REAIM's Pathways to Action codifies and Morocco endorsed in February."
16. **"Why should small states trust a treaty negotiated by weapons states?"** → "Because the text is negotiated by all states, and because capacity-building guarantees participation — that is Package C. Small states are not spectators to this treaty; they are its co-authors."
17. **"Is 'human control' compatible with swarms/drones at scale?"** → "That is precisely what the negotiation must specify: limits on duration, scale and geographic scope of autonomous operation, per the ICRC's framework. The standard is set by law; industry adapts."
18. **"What about AI in nuclear command and control?"** → "Morocco has consistently supported the Assembly's resolutions demanding human control over nuclear command-and-control systems. The same logic applies: human responsibility is non-delegable, at every level of the escalation ladder." (Note: the 2025 resolution on nuclear C2 AI passed 118–9–44 — a useful allied fact.)
19. **"You voted with the US on many things; why should we believe you'll resist them here?"** → "Look at the record, not the assumption: Morocco voted yes on every LAWS resolution while the US moved to no in 2025. Our vote is our answer."
20. **"What is Morocco's single most important demand?"** → "A calendar: a negotiation mandate at the November Review Conference, a defined timeline, and a guaranteed fallback — because the difference between a principle and a law is a date."
