# PART 17 — GOTCHA DEFENSE (THE 10 MOST DANGEROUS QUESTIONS)

1. **"Name one strike your drones conducted in Western Sahara and the human who authorized it."** → Danger: forces you to discuss operational details you cannot/should not. Response: "Operations are governed by national law and IHL; Morocco does not litigate its operations in this forum, nor does any delegation here. What this committee can legislate is the rule: no targeting without human control — a rule Morocco already applies and is asking the world to adopt." Redirect to the mandate. Do NOT provide operational specifics.
2. **"Does the Baykar factory in Morocco export drones to states that violate IHL?"** → "All Moroccan defense exports comply with national law and international obligations, including end-use controls. The honest answer to the systemic question is that the convention should make transparency and end-use safeguards universal — we support that text."
3. **"Isn't the 'two-tier approach' just a euphemism for no ban?"** → "No — it is the difference between prohibition and regulation, a distinction as old as law itself. Uncontrollable and unpredictable systems: prohibited. All others: regulated with binding standards. If the delegate prefers one tier, they should say which systems they would leave unregulated."
4. **"The SG's deadline was 2026 — you've already failed. Why should anyone trust your timeline?"** → "The deadline was set for concluding negotiations that obstruction never allowed to begin. We are not defending a missed date; we are preventing a missed decade. Our timeline — mandate now, text by 2028 — is the honest calendar."
5. **"Morocco chairs COPUOS and hosted an OPCW AI conference — is this about your CV or about Africa?"** → "It is about capacity: the countries that host the mechanisms are the countries that implement the norms. Africa's voice is not a photo opportunity; it is a drafting seat. Morocco's record is proof of the seat, not a substitute for it."
6. **"What would you do if an allied state (US/Israel/Turkey) refused to join the convention?"** → "Negotiate the instrument now, universalize it over time, and hold every state to the standard through review conferences. We do not condition our humanity on another state's signature."
7. **"Isn't Morocco's AI strategy (Mistral, Al-Jazari) creating the very technology you want to ban?"** → "Our strategy is civilian-led — sovereign data, education, industry. And the distinction is the function: civilian and defensive AI under human control is not the subject of the prohibition. The subject is targeting autonomy. We are not banning AI; we are banning machines deciding who dies."
8. **"What is your definition of 'human control' — a human pressing one button per hour?"** → "A human exercising context-appropriate judgment over targeting, with the ability to intervene and terminate, backed by training and accountability — that is the operational standard Morocco applies in the field and the standard the rolling text develops. Rubber-stamping is not control; supervision is."
9. **"Why should Africa trust a process hosted in Geneva where African states were excluded for years?"** → "They were not excluded by text — they were excluded by capacity and by a consensus rule that amplified the loudest. That is why the negotiation structure must guarantee African representation and why capacity-building is a condition, not a courtesy. We will hold that line in the text."
10. **"If your resolution passes and nothing happens in Geneva, you've wasted the committee."** → "The resolution contains its own guarantee: if the Review Conference does not act, the Assembly convenes a conference in 2027. This committee is not asking permission for change; we are scheduling it."
