# PART 11 — ARGUMENT ARSENAL (CLAIM → EVIDENCE → INTEREST → POLICY → SOLUTION)

## 11.1 Ten-second argument (POI/quick intervention)
"The need is proven: 164 states voted for it in December. What is missing is a calendar. Morocco proposes one: a negotiation mandate this November, in Geneva."

## 11.2 Twenty-second argument (short caucus)
"Ten years of negotiation produced principles without law. The GGE's rolling text now contains the elements — characterization, human control, prohibitions, accountability. The African Group, including Morocco, declared in March: we are ready to negotiate on that basis. This committee should demand that the November Review Conference convert elements into a mandate — not more talk."

## 11.3 Thirty-second argument (moderated caucus)
"Autonomy is not the problem; uncontrolled autonomy is. Morocco operates modern drone systems — and precisely for that reason we insist that targeting decisions remain under meaningful human control and that systems incapable of complying with international humanitarian law be prohibited. That is the two-tier approach: prohibit what humanity cannot responsibly use; regulate what it can. The elements are agreed. The mandate is missing. Grant it in November."

## 11.4 Sixty-second argument (formal speech / GSL)
"Mr./Madam Chair — this committee is not being asked to invent a problem. It is being asked to close a gap. The gap is not in our principles — the 2019 Guiding Principles, the Secretary-General's reports, and the GGE's rolling text already articulate them. The gap is between principles and law. Autonomous weapons that select and engage targets without human control raise humanitarian, legal, security and ethical challenges that no voluntary declaration has resolved. The Kingdom of Morocco — a State party to the CCW, a country that hosts and produces modern defense technology, and a member of the African Group that declared its readiness to negotiate in March — believes the elements are mature enough to negotiate. We therefore call on this committee to demand that the Seventh Review Conference of the CCW launch negotiations on a legally binding instrument based on the rolling text, with meaningful human control at its core, African participation and capacity-building guaranteed, and a defined timeline. The machines will not wait; the law must not either."

## 11.6 Deep argument (for debate/framing battles)
**The "who pays the price of delay" argument (economic/humanitarian):** every month without rules, autonomy gets cheaper and proliferates — including to non-state actors (Morocco's own 79/239 submission documents AI-enabled quadcopter threats). The cost of delay is paid by civilians in asymmetric conflicts first; the cost of rules is paid by nobody but the weapons' marginal efficiency. "We are not choosing between security and law; we are choosing between regulated security and unregulated insecurity."
**The "forum" argument:** the CCW is the right home (protocol tradition, expertise, universalizable) *if* consensus cannot become veto. If the Review Conference cannot decide, the GA retains the authority to convene an inclusive diplomatic conference — that is the guarantee clause of your resolution.
**The "human control is practical" argument:** meaningful human control is not anti-technology: it is the operator standard Morocco itself applies (TB2/Akıncı operations are human-commanded); it preserves the military utility of drones while banning the unacceptable class. Regulation of autonomous functions = distinction + proportionality + precaution, operationalized.
**The "anti-personnel prohibition" argument (if pushed):** the ICRC has proposed prohibiting autonomous systems designed or used to target humans; the African Group has repeatedly said systems that cannot be subject to human control should be banned. Morocco can endorse putting this prohibition *on the negotiation agenda* — with the operational point that human-controlled weapons engaging human targets are governed by existing IHL rules (this preserves your drone doctrine).
**The "tech divide" argument (against maximalists and against Bloc B):** "A treaty that only the rich can implement is a treaty that will not be universal. That is why capacity-building is not a footnote — it is a condition of the convention's legitimacy."
