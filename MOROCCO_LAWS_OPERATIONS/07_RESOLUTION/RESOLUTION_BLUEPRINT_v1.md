## DRAFT RESOLUTION — "Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems"

**Sponsors:** Austria, Brazil, Mexico, Morocco, Sierra Leone, Türkiye
**Signatories:** Chile, Costa Rica, Germany, Ireland, Kazakhstan, Malawi, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, South Africa

### Preambulatory (recall-only; 7 PPs)

*The General Assembly,*

*PP1 [VERIFIED]* Recalling its resolutions 78/241 of 22 December 2023, 79/62 of 2 December 2024 and 80/57 of 1 December 2025 on lethal autonomous weapons systems, **[Q7]**

*PP2* Guided by the purposes and principles of the Charter of the United Nations, including the maintenance of international peace and security and the promotion of the peaceful settlement of disputes,

*PP3 [VERIFIED]* Recalling the Convention on Prohibitions or Restrictions on the Use of Certain Conventional Weapons Which May Be Deemed to Be Excessively Injurious or to Have Indiscriminate Effects (CCW), its 2019 Guiding Principles, and the work of the Group of Governmental Experts on Emerging Technologies in the Area of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems, including its rolling text,

*PP4 [VERIFIED]* Taking note of the report of the Secretary-General (A/79/88) and the joint call of the Secretary-General and the President of the International Committee of the Red Cross for the urgent negotiation of a legally binding instrument, **[Q7]**

*PP5* Expressing grave concern at the possible negative consequences and impact of autonomous weapons systems on global security and regional and international stability, including the risk of an emerging arms race, the lowering of the threshold for conflict and escalation, and proliferation to unauthorized recipients, including non-State actors, **[Q1][Q6]**

*PP6* Affirming that international law, including international humanitarian law and international human rights law, applies to all weapons systems, and reaffirming that any weapon which cannot be used in compliance with international humanitarian law must not be used, **[Q4]**

*PP7* Recognizing the dual-use nature of artificial intelligence technologies, the significant civilian benefits of AI, and the right of all States to develop and use technology for peaceful purposes in accordance with international law, **[Q9]**

### Operative (9 OPs — one per guide question, plus mandate)

*OP1 [Q1]* *Affirms* the urgent need for a legally binding international instrument on autonomous weapons systems, built on a two-tier approach: **prohibiting** weapons systems that select and engage targets without meaningful human control or that cannot be used in compliance with international humanitarian law, and **regulating** all other autonomous weapons systems through binding standards on design, testing, deployment and use;

*OP2 [Q1][Q7]* *Calls upon* the High Contracting Parties to the CCW to adopt, at the Seventh Review Conference of the Convention, to be held from 16 to 20 November 2026 in Geneva, a mandate to negotiate a legally binding instrument on the basis of the rolling text of the Group of Governmental Experts, with a view to concluding negotiations by 2028;

*OP3 [Q7]* *Decides* that, in the event that no negotiation mandate is adopted by the Seventh Review Conference, the General Assembly shall convene an inclusive diplomatic conference in 2027, open to all Member States, observer States, the International Committee of the Red Cross, and relevant civil society and scientific experts, to negotiate such an instrument;

*OP4 [Q2]* *Calls upon* all States to work, within the negotiation process, toward a common international definition of "meaningful human control" based on the elements contained in the GGE rolling text, including context-appropriate human judgment over targeting, predictability, explainability, the ability to intervene and terminate operations, and accountability; and requests the Secretary-General to compile and transmit to Member States existing national definitions and implementation practices to inform this work;

*OP5 [Q3]* *Affirms* that responsibility and accountability for the use of force can never be transferred to machines, and *calls upon* all States to ensure, at the national level, that legal reviews of new weapons systems include autonomous functions, that testing, evaluation, validation and verification are conducted prior to deployment, and that audit trails and chains of command preserve individual accountability;

*OP6 [Q4]* *Calls upon* all States to ensure that any autonomous weapons system used in armed conflict is capable of being used in accordance with the principles of distinction, proportionality and precaution, and to refrain, pending the entry into force of the instrument, from the use of systems that select and engage targets without meaningful human control;

*OP7 [Q5]* *Calls upon* all States to enhance transparency and confidence-building by exchanging national policies on autonomy in weapons systems, publishing summaries of legal reviews without prejudice to national security, and engaging in dialogues on responsible military use of artificial intelligence;

*OP8 [Q8]* *Calls upon* all States to adopt and enforce national export and transfer controls for autonomous weapons technologies and their components, including end-use and end-user safeguards, and to take measures to prevent acquisition of such systems by non-State actors;

*OP9 [Q6]* *Requests* the United Nations Office for Disarmament Affairs and the United Nations Institute for Disarmament Research, in cooperation with the African Union and other relevant regional organizations, to establish a technical assistance and capacity-building programme for developing States — including legal review training, policy drafting support and testing expertise; *invites* Member States to contribute voluntary funding to a dedicated trust fund; and *encourages* South–South cooperation in this regard;

*OP10 [Q9]* *Affirms* that nothing in this resolution shall be construed to restrict civilian AI innovation, research and development, or the use of artificial intelligence for intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance, logistics, cybersecurity and decision-support functions that do not involve autonomous targeting without meaningful human control;

*OP11 [Q7]* *Requests* the Secretary-General to report to the General Assembly at its eighty-first session on the outcome of the Seventh Review Conference and on the implementation of the present resolution, and *decides* to include in the provisional agenda of its eighty-first session the item entitled "Lethal autonomous weapons systems."

## NEGOTIATION MAP (what each clause costs/earns)
- **OP2 + OP3 = the package deal.** If the US bloc attacks OP2 (mandate), offer OP3 as the fallback — they cannot oppose both without opposing their own transparency agenda. **Never trade OP2 alone.**
- **OP4** = Türkiye + India + Pakistan's price (definitional clarity). Non-negotiable for them; keep.
- **OP5** = UK/US-leaning delegates' price (they already do legal reviews). Makes them co-owners.
- **OP7** = literally the US Political Declaration's content. Their own words. **Your trap for the US bloc.**
- **OP8** = China/Israel will resist (export controls) — tradeable for their abstention-not-opposition; soften to "in accordance with national laws and international obligations" if needed.
- **OP9** = the Global South's price. Non-negotiable. The entire African/NAM/OIC bloc votes on this clause.
- **OP10** = the innovation camp's price (China, US, India, Japan). Protects them. Keep — it buys abstentions.

## AMENDMENT TRAPS TO WATCH
1. **"Voluntary" injection:** someone will move to insert "voluntary" before "instrument" in OP1 → kill via friendly amendment: "recognizing that voluntary measures are complementary, the Assembly affirms the need for a legally binding instrument..." (pre-negotiated with Austria).
2. **Definitional stall:** motion to delete OP4's definitional work ("too hard to define") → counter: "the guide's own question 2 demands it; the delegate who cannot define human control cannot claim to practice it."
3. **Scope expansion:** amendment adding "all autonomous systems, including surveillance" → counters OP10's savings clause; fight with OP1's two-tier + OP10.
4. **Procedural kill:** motion to table/divide OP2 → invoke OP3 as the guarantee; force recorded vote.
