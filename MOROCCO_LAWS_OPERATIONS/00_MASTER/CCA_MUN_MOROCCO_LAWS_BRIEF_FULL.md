# PART 1 — EXECUTIVE STRATEGY (THE BOTTOM LINE)

**1. Your one-sentence doctrine:** "The Kingdom of Morocco, a State party to the CCW and a responsible operator of modern drone technology, supports the urgent conclusion of a legally binding instrument on autonomous weapons systems built on the two-tier approach — prohibitions for systems that cannot operate with meaningful human control or in compliance with IHL, and regulation of all others — and calls on this committee to trigger the negotiation mandate at the CCW Seventh Review Conference in November 2026."

**2. Your strategic identity:** **The Bridge. The African Drafter. The Operator's Voice.** Morocco is one of the few delegations in the room that sits in *three* circles at once: (a) the African Group / NAM / OIC majority that demands a binding instrument; (b) the US-led "responsible military AI" declaration circle (Morocco is an endorser); (c) the community of states that *actually operate armed drones* (TB2/Akıncı fleet, Baykar production facility in Morocco). That triangulation is your power: you can translate between the "ban now" bloc, the "voluntary first" bloc, and the "we need practical rules" producers' bloc. No other delegate can credibly hold all three lanes. Use it.

**3. The answer to the agenda question:** The "need" for a binding convention is **already established** — 164 states voted for A/RES/80/57 in December 2025, the SG and ICRC demand it, the African Group demands it, the GGE's rolling text already contains the elements. The real debate is **not whether, but how, where, and when**. Your job is to frame the committee's work as: *"The need is proven. This committee must convert ten years of consensus elements into a negotiation mandate — starting at the CCW Seventh Review Conference (16–20 Nov 2026) — with a defined timeline, an inclusive forum, and African capacity-building built in."*

**4. Your three leadable policy packages (the "Morocco Three"):**
- **Package A — "Start Now" Negotiation Mandate:** resolution calls on CCW High Contracting Parties to adopt, at the Seventh Review Conference, a mandate to negotiate a legally binding instrument on the basis of the GGE rolling text, with a target to conclude by 2028 (realistic re-timing of the SG's 2026 benchmark), and — if consensus cannot be reached at the Review Conference — convene an inclusive UNGA-convened diplomatic conference as a fallback track.
- **Package B — "Human Control Is the Standard" (substantive core):** endorsement of the two-tier approach; prohibition of systems that select and engage targets without meaningful human control or that cannot comply with IHL; positive obligations (predictability, explainability, accountability, training); no autonomy for targeting decisions against persons.
- **Package C — "Africa Is Not Left Behind":** capacity-building and technical assistance mechanism (UNIDIR/UNODA-coordinated, voluntary funding, South–South cooperation), support for universalization of the CCW, transparency/export safeguards that do not deny developing states lawful access to defensive drone technology, and African participation in all negotiation structures.

**5. Your red lines (non-negotiable):**
- Nothing in the resolution that could be twisted into a precedent against Morocco's territorial integrity (Western Sahara). Reject any singling-out of states or regions.

- No "human control" standard so vague it becomes a checkbox, and no standard so rigid it bans defensive ISR/logistics/cyber AI.
- No funding mechanism that shifts costs to developing states without donor commitments.

**6. Biggest threat to you personally:** the "hypocrisy attack" — *"Morocco preaches human control while its drones strike in Western Sahara, and it builds them with Turkey/Israel/China."* It is survivable and even convertible (see Part 12, Attack #1) — you regulate what you operate; your entire national security argument is that *human-controlled* drones are the lawful alternative to *autonomous* ones.

## 2.2 What the UNGA can and cannot do — know this cold
- `[VERIFIED FACT]` UN Charter Articles 10–14: the GA may **discuss and recommend** on disarmament and international security; recommendations are **non-binding** on states.
- `[VERIFIED FACT]` A General Assembly resolution **cannot itself create a binding treaty**. A convention is binding only when states negotiate and ratify it (Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties logic: signature/ratification/accession).
- What the GA **can** do, and what your resolution should do:
  1. **Declare/evaluate** the need (your agenda item);
  2. **Call upon** states to negotiate (political mandate);
  3. **Convene or recommend** a diplomatic conference or negotiation track (precedent: the GA convened the conferences that produced the TPNW 2017, the CCW 1980, the Ottawa Convention 1997, the Arms Trade Treaty 2013);
  4. **Request** reports from the Secretary-General;
  5. **Establish/request** mechanisms, studies, expert groups, capacity-building programs (funded via assessed/voluntary routes — be precise about which);
  6. **Endorse** the work of the CCW GGE and its rolling text.
- `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]` Any resolution clause that "bans X" as if this committee had legislative power is a drafting error your opponents will expose. Phrase as: "decides to convene...", "calls upon all States...", "urges the High Contracting Parties...", "recommends that negotiations commence...". The **real legal instrument is the future treaty**; this committee's job is the **negotiation mandate**.

# PART 5 — TOPIC INTELLIGENCE (DEEP ISSUE ANALYSIS)

## 5.1 What LAWS are — the working characterization (use this, it's consensus)
- `[VERIFIED FACT]` GGE rolling text characterization of a lethal autonomous weapon: *"a functionally integrated combination of one or more weapons and technological components, that can identify, select, and engage a target, without intervention by a human operator in the execution of these tasks."* Clarifications: (A) a human operator setting target parameters does not exclude a system from the characterization; (B) systems that only damage/destroy objects or injure (non-lethal use) are not excluded; (C) without prejudice to future modification.
- `[VERIFIED FACT]` The 2019 CCW 11 Guiding Principles: IHL applies; human responsibility cannot be transferred to machines; human-machine interaction must ensure human control; accountability; risk assessments; security/non-proliferation safeguards; etc.
- **Definitional flashpoints in debate:** (1) "lethal" in the name; (2) whether "identification" is included with "selection and engagement"; (3) "meaningful human control" vs "appropriate levels of human judgment/control" vs "human-machine interaction" — three rival formulations; (4) whether the instrument is a Protocol VI under the CCW or a standalone convention.

## 5.2 Root causes → mechanism → effect (map for your speeches)
- **Root cause 1: The AI/robotics technology curve.** Sensing, computer vision, and machine learning made autonomous target selection technically feasible and cheap. *Effect:* autonomy migrates from missile guidance (decades old) to wide-area strike/swarm systems. *International consequence:* proliferation of drone autonomy to non-state actors (verified concern in Morocco's own 79/239 submission).
- **Root cause 2: Strategic competition.** US, Russia, China, and middle powers are racing on military AI (US DoD Directive 3000.09; Russia's rhetoric on AI weapons; China's public support for banning fully autonomous systems). *Effect:* arms-race dynamics, fear of being left behind, secrecy. *Consequence:* states hesitate to bind themselves.
- **Root cause 3: Institutional design failure.** The CCW works by consensus; a small minority (historically US, Russia, India, Israel, UK, ROK among others) blocked progress for a decade. *Effect:* GGE outputs are lowest-common-denominator; "elements" without "negotiations." *Consequence:* parallel tracks (UNGA resolutions, Vienna, REAIM, regional conferences) emerged — momentum without a mandate.
- **Root cause 4: IHL's human-centric design.** Distinction, proportionality, precaution were built for human decision-making (`[VERIFIED FACT]` — this is Morocco's own 79/239 argument). *Effect:* opaque ("black-box") systems may be incapable of IHL compliance in practice. *Consequence:* ICRC (March 2026 paper) calls for prohibiting unpredictable systems and anti-personnel autonomous systems; HRW (April 2025) adds human-rights violations (right to life, dignity, non-discrimination, digital dehumanization).

# PART 13 — SPEECH STRATEGY (OPENING, GSL, MODERATED CAUCUS)

## 13.1 Opening speech (90 seconds) — Strategic Identity: "The Bridge That Drafts"
> Mr./Madam Chair, distinguished delegates —
>
> We are asked to evaluate the need for a binding convention on lethal autonomous weapons. Allow the Kingdom of Morocco to answer as a country that does not observe this debate from a distance: Morocco is a State party to the CCW, a member of the African Group that declared itself ready to negotiate on the GGE's rolling text last March, an endorser of responsible-use frameworks — and an operator and future producer of modern drone technology. We say this openly, because credibility begins with honesty.
>
> Our conclusion is that the need is not in question. It was settled by 164 votes in this Assembly in December, by the Secretary-General and the ICRC, and by the reality that targeting autonomy is becoming cheaper, faster, and more accessible — including to non-state actors. What is missing is not analysis. It is a calendar.
>
> The elements of an instrument exist. The two-tier approach — prohibiting systems that cannot operate under meaningful human control or in compliance with international humanitarian law, and regulating all others — commands the consensus of this room. What has been blocked for a decade is the decision to negotiate.
>
> Morocco therefore proposes three commitments for this committee: **first**, to demand that the CCW Seventh Review Conference in November launch negotiations on the basis of the rolling text, with a defined timeline; **second**, to enshrine meaningful human control as the core standard, with prohibitions for systems that cannot meet it; **third**, to guarantee that Africa and the developing world are builders of this regime — through capacity-building, participation, and access — not spectators to it.
>
> Morocco regulates what it operates, and we ask every delegation that operates technology to do the same. The machines are not waiting. Neither should we. Thank you.

**Strategic notes:** the phrase to associate with your delegation: **"A calendar, not more analysis."** Second phrase: **"Regulate what you operate."** Claim ownership of: the negotiation timeline; the bridge role; African drafting.

# PART 15 — RESOLUTION STRATEGY (POLICY PACKAGES, CLAUSE BANK, RED LINES)

## 15.1 The Morocco Three — FAMMS-tested
**Package A — "Start Now" negotiation mandate**
- *Problem:* ten years of elements, zero negotiation mandate.
- *Mechanism:* OP calls upon CCW HCPs to adopt at the 7th Review Conference a mandate to negotiate a legally binding instrument on the basis of the GGE rolling text; decides that if no consensus mandate emerges by 31 December 2026, the GA will convene an inclusive diplomatic conference in 2027; requests the SG to report on both tracks.
- *Funding:* negligible (existing processes); conference costs via regular budget/voluntary contributions — name it.
- *Authority:* GA Articles 10–14 + CCW HCP decision-making. Feasible: yes — mirrors the real 42-state demand. Measurable: Review Conference outcome + SG report. Safeguards: inclusive participation; no prejudice to CCW.
- *Timeline:* Nov 2026 decision; negotiations 2027–2028; interim review 2027.

**Package B — "Human control is the standard" (substantive core)**
- *Mechanism:* endorses the two-tier approach; calls on states to prohibit/ensure prohibition of systems that select and engage targets without meaningful human control or in a manner incapable of IHL compliance; affirms positive obligations (predictability, explainability, accountability, training, legal review, T&E); puts the anti-personnel autonomous systems question on the negotiation agenda; savings clause for non-lethal AI (ISR/logistics/cyber/decision-support).
- *Authority:* consistent with IHL (Additional Protocol I rules), CCW principles, A/RES/80/57.

**Package C — "Africa is not left behind"**
- *Mechanism:* requests UNODA/UNIDIR, in cooperation with the AU, to establish a technical assistance and capacity-building program for developing states (national legal reviews, policy drafting, T&E skills); invites voluntary contributions; encourages South–South cooperation (Morocco offers concrete cooperation, mirroring its IAEA/OPCW practice); calls for universalization of the CCW; ensures African representation in any negotiation structure.
- *Funding:* voluntary contributions + existing UNODA/UNIDIR trust funds — realistic and named. Measurable: program report to the 82nd session.
