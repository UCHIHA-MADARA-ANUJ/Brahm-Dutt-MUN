## ⭐ PART C — 4 NEW 60-SECOND SPEECHES (just in case)

**C-9 — Moratorium / interim measures:**
"Mr./Madam Chair — the guide to our work asks what we do while negotiations proceed. Morocco's answer: interim self-restraint. Pending the entry into force of the instrument, we call on all States to refrain from the development and use of weapons systems that select and engage targets without meaningful human control, and to declare such restraint nationally. This is not a ban; it is a pause with a purpose — verifiable in declaration, reversible by law, and costing no lawful capability. Every human-controlled system remains available to every State that needs it. A moratorium without a negotiation mandate is a gesture; ours comes with a calendar. Let the pause begin in this room and end in Geneva. Thank you."

**C-10 — Anti-personnel prohibition:**
"Mr./Madam Chair — the ICRC has identified the clearest line in this debate: autonomous weapons designed or used to apply force against persons. The African Group has said that systems which cannot be subject to human control must be banned. Malawi has said that weapons which target people must be prohibited, for legal and ethical reasons. Morocco agrees — and we place that prohibition at the top of the negotiation agenda. Some will say we must not pre-judge negotiations. We are not prejudging; we are prioritizing. The negotiators must answer one question first: may a machine select a human being as its target? Morocco's answer is no — and we believe the public conscience has already answered."

**C-11 — Human rights dimension:**
"Mr./Madam Chair — this debate is not only about the laws of war; it is about the right to life. A delegation reminded this Assembly that autonomous systems fundamentally challenge the right to life by removing human judgment from lethal force decisions. Human Rights Watch has documented risks to life, privacy, assembly and remedy. A machine that reduces a person to a target profile degrades the dignity that human rights law exists to protect. The convention must therefore be anchored in international human rights law as well as international humanitarian law — and our text says so. Law protects life. The question is whether we write the law before the machine writes the outcome."

**C-12 — Autonomous weapons in current conflicts:**
"Mr./Madam Chair — to the delegate who says autonomous weapons are hypothetical, allow me to read from the record. The UN Panel of Experts documented autonomous attack by loitering munitions in Libya in 2020. Ukraine has reported autonomous-mode strikes by AI-controlled quadcopters. In January, a twenty-drone autonomous swarm was live-fired in a coordinated strike. AI-enabled targeting systems have been reported in this year's strikes in the Middle East. Autonomous weapons are not hypothetical — they are in Libya's report, Ukraine's sky, and the test ranges of 2026. The only hypothetical is the law. That is what we are here to end."
