# ⚙️ PROCEDURE QUICK SHEET — STANDARD UNGA SCHOOL-MUN (SESSION 6)
**Rule:** This is standard UNGA practice. YOUR conference's Rules of Procedure control — if the chair rules differently, follow the chair. Day-1 first move: confirm with the chair ("Mr./Madam Chair, may I confirm this committee operates under standard UNGA rules of procedure?" — a procedural-competence flex).

## 1. THE TOOLBOX (know all of these)
| Tool | What it does | When YOU use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Motion to set agenda** | Adopts/amends the agenda | Day 1: support the given agenda (it's your advantage). |
| **Motion for moderated caucus** | "Topic + speaking time + total time" — the workhorse | **YOUR MAIN WEAPON.** Motion YOUR subtopics: negotiation mandate (90s total/30s speaking), two-tier (120s/30s), human control (120s/45s), capacity building (90s/30s). |
| **Motion for unmoderated caucus** | Free negotiation, X minutes | The moment a draft needs sponsors or a merger needs to happen. You call it. |
| **Motion to extend debate / close debate** | Timing control | Extend when your side is winning; close when the rival needs more time. |
| **Motion to table** | Postpone a draft/amendment | Kill a poison amendment (rare; know your conference's rules). |
| **Motion to divide the question** | Vote on clauses separately | Split a poisoned amalgam — your best procedural weapon. |
| **Motion to introduce a working paper** | Formalizes your draft | Your blueprint, day 1. |
| **Motion to move into voting procedure** | Ends debate, votes | When your numbers are locked. |
| **Point of order** | Rules violation/relevance | Only for genuine violations; never abuse. |
| **Point of inquiry** | Ask the chair a rules question | "May I ask whether amendments require a second?" |
| **Point of personal privilege** | Comfort (audibility etc.) | Rarely; never for theater. |
| **Point of information (POI)** | Question to the speaker (if rules allow) | Your POI_BANK_20. |
| **Right of reply** | Response to a personal attack | Max 2; never on substance. |
| **Yield (if GSL)** | To chair / to another delegate / to questions | Yield to questions when your speech is provocation-ready; to chair otherwise. |

## 2. SPEAKING-TIME MATH (confirmed: GSL 90s, mod 60s)
- 90 seconds ≈ **230 words** (opening speech is ~215 — fits).
- 60 seconds ≈ **150 words** (your 8 mod speeches are ~150–155 — fits).
- 30 seconds ≈ 75 words (interventions during other caucuses).
- **Speak 5–7 times minimum across two days.** Dais math: speech score scales with count × quality.

## 3. VOTING (standard UNGA school practice — confirm)
- **Procedural votes:** simple majority of members present and voting; no abstentions allowed (procedural motions pass/fail by yes/no).
- **Substantive votes (resolutions/amendments):** simple majority unless your RoP says 2/3 for "important questions" (some conferences do — confirm day 1). Abstentions don't count as yes/no; you need majority of those voting.
- **Amendments:** usually require 20% of members or a second + a minimum number of sponsors (e.g., 1/5 of committee); friendly amendments (all sponsors agree) pass without vote in many conferences; unfriendly need a vote.
- **Division of question:** if adopted, each divided clause votes separately; then the surviving clauses vote as a whole.
- **Placard math:** your bloc target = 120+ yes. Even 2/3 of ~40 delegates in a school committee = ~27 — your coalition plan covers it.

## 4. ORDER OF PRECEDENCE (standard)
1. Point of order → 2. Point of personal privilege → 3. Point of inquiry → 4. Motion to adjourn/close debate → 5. Motion to table → 6. Motion for unmoderated caucus → 7. Motion for moderated caucus → (then other motions; check your RoP).
**Procedural motions are non-debatable and decided by the chair/vote immediately** — know this so rivals can't filibuster your motions.

## 5. YOUR PROCEDURAL GAME PLAN
- **Day 1, first caucus slot:** motion "moderated caucus: the negotiation mandate and timeline for the CCW Review Conference" (60s/30s). You speak last for synthesis.
- **After rival tables a poison amendment:** motion to divide the question → separate vote isolates their poison.
- **When your numbers are locked:** motion to move into voting procedure (denies them stall time).
- **Never:** motion things you don't need; challenge the chair publicly; use points as theater. Each procedural tool used well = one visible competence mark for the dais.

## 6. THE CHAIR-INTERACTION RULES
- Address the chair first and last in every speech: "Mr./Madam Chair."
- When recognized: stand, state motion/point in one sentence, sit.
- Never interrupt the chair. Never argue a chair ruling — use right of reply/appeal per RoP if genuinely wrong.
- Bring the chair your final text early ("for the record") — chairs remember who submitted first.
