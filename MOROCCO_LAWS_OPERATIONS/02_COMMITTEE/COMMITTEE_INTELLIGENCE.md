## 2.2 What the UNGA can and cannot do — know this cold
- `[VERIFIED FACT]` UN Charter Articles 10–14: the GA may **discuss and recommend** on disarmament and international security; recommendations are **non-binding** on states.
- `[VERIFIED FACT]` A General Assembly resolution **cannot itself create a binding treaty**. A convention is binding only when states negotiate and ratify it (Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties logic: signature/ratification/accession).
- What the GA **can** do, and what your resolution should do:
  1. **Declare/evaluate** the need (your agenda item);
  2. **Call upon** states to negotiate (political mandate);
  3. **Convene or recommend** a diplomatic conference or negotiation track (precedent: the GA convened the conferences that produced the TPNW 2017, the CCW 1980, the Ottawa Convention 1997, the Arms Trade Treaty 2013);
  4. **Request** reports from the Secretary-General;
  5. **Establish/request** mechanisms, studies, expert groups, capacity-building programs (funded via assessed/voluntary routes — be precise about which);
  6. **Endorse** the work of the CCW GGE and its rolling text.
- `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]` Any resolution clause that "bans X" as if this committee had legislative power is a drafting error your opponents will expose. Phrase as: "decides to convene...", "calls upon all States...", "urges the High Contracting Parties...", "recommends that negotiations commence...". The **real legal instrument is the future treaty**; this committee's job is the **negotiation mandate**.

## 2.4 The committee battlefield (standard UNGA/MUN procedure — verify against your conference's RoP)
Standard First Committee-style flow you should assume (and adapt): Roll call → Setting the agenda (usually by acclamation) → General Speakers' List (GSL) → Moderated caucuses (motion: topic + speaking time + total time) → Unmoderated caucuses → Working papers → Draft resolutions (sponsors + signatories) → Amendments (friendly/unfriendly) → Voting procedure (substantive; may require 2/3 for important questions under some RoP — check yours) → Explanations of vote.
- **Motions to master:** motion to set the agenda; motion for moderated caucus (with a *specific* subtopic — this is your main steering weapon); motion for unmoderated caucus; motion to introduce/table a working paper; motion to move into voting procedure; motion to divide the question (rare, powerful); right of reply; point of order; point of information (if your rules allow POIs during speeches — know whether yes/no); yields (to another delegate / to questions / to the Chair).
- `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]` Every motion you make must have a purpose: topic control, bloc signaling, or procedural timing. Never motion for the sake of motion.
- **Voting math:** 193 member states; majority of members present and voting. The pro-instrument majority in the real world is ~164; your simulated committee will be similar in shape. Assume the resolution passes; **the fight is over the content, the sponsors' list, and the narrative** — and over your personal visibility.

## 2.5 What this committee cannot do (do not propose it)
- Cannot impose the convention on states (no universal jurisdiction clause as if binding here);
- Cannot create UN funding obligations without Fifth Committee action — use "requests the Secretary-General to establish... funded by voluntary contributions" or "invites Member States to contribute";
- Cannot sanction or enforce (that is Security Council territory under Chapter VII);
- Cannot amend the CCW from here (only High Contracting Parties can) — it can only **call upon** them;
- Cannot adjudicate compliance (no court; treaty review conferences do that later).
