## ✅ THE SPEECH

> **Article 36 of Additional Protocol One requires states to conduct a legal review of every new weapon before deployment. Morocco ratified that obligation in 2011. But a review conducted once — before a system enters the battlefield — is a snapshot. An autonomous system that uses machine learning is not a photograph. It is a living system that adapts, drifts, and evolves after deployment. The weapon you reviewed in the laboratory is not the weapon operating on the battlefield six months later.**
>
> Honourable Chair, this is not a theoretical risk. Machine learning systems update their own parameters based on real-world data. Training datasets degrade. Adversarial inputs corrupt targeting models. Software updates introduce new behaviours that were never reviewed. A one-time, pre-deployment review cannot guarantee compliance for a system that changes after it is fielded. The GGE itself concluded that states must ensure compliance *"throughout the lifecycle of a weapon system"* *(2023 GGE report).* Yet most states conduct their Article 36 reviews in secret, with no international standard, and never revisit them.
>
> Of the 174 states parties to Additional Protocol One, only 12 to 15 are even known to have a weapons review mechanism *(SIPRI, 2015).* The United States keeps its reviews classified. Russia opposes any new limitations. They call existing law sufficient — but existing law does not require a second look at a weapon that has learned, adapted, and changed since it was first approved.
>
> Morocco therefore proposes mandatory lifecycle review: an initial Article 36 review before deployment, periodic reassessment after fielding, and mandatory reporting of any software update or behavioural change that could affect legal compliance. Reviews must be transparent — not hidden behind national security. A weapon that evolves in secret is a weapon no one governs.
>
> **A one-time review for a lifetime weapon is not oversight. It is a rubber stamp. Morocco yields.**
